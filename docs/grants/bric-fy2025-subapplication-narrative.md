1.0 Executive Summary
This subapplication is submitted to the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) under the Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities (BRIC) program, Fiscal Year 2025 Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO), on behalf of Anthony John Tucker, owner of record at the restricted site, Point Township, Posey County, Indiana 47620 (Parcel ID: 65-09-35-200-001.000-009). The proposed project — the Point Township Integrated Flood Resilience System, Archimedes Line Phase I — is a multi-component engineered flood control initiative designed to protect approximately 2,400 acres of agricultural land and an estimated 340 residential and commercial structures within Point Township from chronic and catastrophic Ohio River and Wabash River backwater flooding.
Point Township occupies the lowest topographic position in Posey County, Indiana, situated within the tri-state confluence zone of the Ohio and Wabash Rivers at the Indiana/Illinois/Kentucky border — a region that has sustained four (4) Presidentially declared major disaster declarations attributable to riverine flooding (FEMA DR-596, DR-891, DR-1109, and DR-3238). The project site lies within FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA) Zone A, FIRM Panel 18129C0225D, with a Base Flood Elevation (BFE) of 375.0 ft NAVD88 and a current ground surface elevation of 377.2 ft NAVD88 — providing only 2.2 ft of freeboard above the established BFE.
The Archimedes Line Phase I project proposes construction of engineered earthen berms, automated drainage control structures, engineered wet retention basins, expansion of the existing Point Township Digital Twin (PTDT v32) real-time sensor network, and outfall stabilization at the Ohio River backwater interface. The total estimated project cost ranges from $3,500,000 to $7,000,000, with a midpoint cost of $5,250,000 used for benefit-cost analysis purposes. The calculated Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR) is 1.41, which exceeds the FEMA minimum threshold of 1.0 by 41%. Federal funding is requested at the standard 75% cost share ($2,625,000–$5,250,000), with the applicant eligible to request elevated 90/10 cost-share treatment as a small impoverished community. This subapplication is submitted through Indiana's eGrants system to the Indiana Department of Homeland Security (IDHS), serving as the State Hazard Mitigation Officer (SHMO), prior to the deadline of July 23, 2026, at 3:00 PM Eastern Time.

3.0 Problem Statement and Community Vulnerability Assessment
3.1 Regional Flood Hazard Context
Posey County, Indiana and Point Township in particular are situated within one of the most chronically and severely flood-impacted geographic zones in the continental United States: the tri-state confluence zone of the Ohio River and the Wabash River at the Indiana/Illinois/Kentucky border. This region is subject to multi-directional riverine flood threats arising from the simultaneous high-stage events on both river systems — a compound flooding dynamic that renders conventional single-source flood modeling insufficient and that dramatically amplifies inundation risk beyond what either river alone would produce.
Point Township occupies the lowest topographic position within Posey County, rendering it the primary recipient of floodwaters during Ohio River and Wabash River concurrent high-water events. When both rivers achieve elevated stage simultaneously — a condition that occurs with increasing frequency under evolving climate conditions — backwater effects from the Ohio River mainstem propagate inland through the natural drainage network, overtopping low-lying agricultural land, inundating residential structures, and disrupting critical rural infrastructure for periods ranging from days to weeks.
Modern climate science and hydrologic modeling indicate that the historical 100-year flood frequency is compressing toward 25–50 year recurrence intervals under projected future precipitation and snowmelt regimes, meaning that flood events once expected once per century are now anticipated to occur two to four times as frequently within the design life of infrastructure assets. This compression of recurrence intervals substantially amplifies annualized expected damages and strengthens the case for proactive, mitigation-first investment of the type proposed under this subapplication.

4.0 Proposed Project Description — Archimedes Line Phase I
4.1 Project Overview and Design Philosophy
The Archimedes Line is a comprehensive engineered flood control and drainage management system designed to intercept Ohio River backwater inflow before it can enter Point Township's interior drainage network, thereby eliminating the primary inundation pathway that threatens approximately 2,400 acres of agricultural land and an estimated 340 residential and commercial structures within Point Township. The name "Archimedes Line" derives from Archimedes' principle of displacement — reflecting the project's core hydrologic strategy: removing flood volume from the active floodplain system by capturing, retaining, and controlled-releasing excess water through purpose-built infrastructure rather than allowing uncontrolled overland sheet flow to damage structures and crops.
Phase I of the Archimedes Line system, as described in this subapplication, constitutes the foundational infrastructure required to achieve meaningful flood risk reduction within the project area. Phase I is deliberately designed to be independently functional — i.e., the Phase I scope delivers measurable, auditable benefits without dependence on subsequent phases — while also establishing the physical and digital infrastructure upon which future phases will build.
4.2 Component 1 — Archimedes Berm System
The primary structural element of Phase I is the construction of compacted clay earthen berms ranging from 3 to 6 feet in height, positioned along the primary backwater ingress corridors identified through USGS 3DEP LiDAR digital elevation analysis and PTDT v32 hydrologic modeling. The berms are designed to a target crest elevation of 379.8 ft NAVD88, representing 4.8 feet above the current FEMA Base Flood Elevation (375.0 ft NAVD88) and 2.6 feet above the site's current ground elevation of 377.2 ft NAVD88.
Berm construction materials consist of compacted clay sourced through the USACE Section 204 Beneficial Use of Dredged Material program (see Section 7.3), providing clean clay fill at negligible material cost. The berm alignment follows the NW low-lying depression zones identified by PTDT v32 terrain analysis as the highest-volume backwater ingress corridors. Geotechnical design specifies a minimum Factor of Safety (FS) against slope failure of 1.65, exceeding the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) minimum standard of 1.40.
4.3 Component 2 — Drainage Control Structures
Two (2) automated tide gate assemblies will be installed at the primary drainage channel crossings where interior agricultural drainage ditches intersect the backwater ingress corridor. Each tide gate assembly is designed to operate in two modes: (1) gravity-flow drainage during non-flood conditions, allowing normal agricultural drainage to proceed unimpeded; and (2) automated closure during flood conditions, preventing Ohio River backwater from propagating inland through the drainage channel network.
Gate operators consist of electric actuators with manual override capability, integrated with the PTDT v32 SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition) telemetry system. Automatic gate operation is triggered by real-time sensor readings from the PT sensor network and, upon implementation of Phase I Component 4, by NWS Evansville (PAH) automated flood alert signals. Manual override capability is maintained at each gate structure in compliance with FEMA performance standards for critical flood control infrastructure.
4.4 Component 3 — Retention Basin Network
Two (2) engineered wet retention basins will be constructed within the NW low-lying depression zones identified by USGS 3DEP LiDAR analysis and PTDT v32 hydrologic modeling. Each basin is designed to an approximate surface area of 3–5 acres (target: approximately 4 acres each), providing combined temporary storage capacity calculated to receive 100-year storm runoff as defined by NOAA Atlas 14 design storm parameters for Posey County, Indiana.
Basin design has been developed using the EPA Storm Water Management Model (SWMM 5.2 solver), with NOAA Atlas 14 design storm inputs (100-year, 24-hour precipitation frequency) for Posey County, Indiana. Basin outfall structures are designed to regulate discharge rate to pre-development levels, preventing downstream channel capacity exceedance and complying with Indiana 327 IAC 15-13 post-construction stormwater runoff control requirements. Each basin will be designed to provide secondary ecological benefit, consistent with the USACE NWP 27 Aquatic Habitat Restoration permitting pathway described in Section 6.0.
4.5 Component 4 — PTDT v32 Early Warning System Expansion
The existing Point Township Digital Twin (PTDT v32) sensor network, currently operational at nodes PT-001 through PT-009, will be expanded by nine (9) additional telemetry nodes (PT-010 through PT-018) at strategic inundation-risk locations within the project watershed. The expanded network will provide continuous real-time monitoring of water level, flow velocity, soil saturation, and berm structural performance across the full Archimedes Line Phase I project footprint.
Each new sensor node (PT-010 through PT-018) will transmit data to the PTDT v32 PostgreSQL audit ledger maintained at the project anchor site, with all simulation outputs recorded under SHA256 Daubert-compliant immutable hash chain protocols to ensure data integrity and legal admissibility of all monitoring records. Integration with the NWS Evansville (PAH) Automated Flood Alert System will enable automated pre-storm gate closure commands and community alert notifications, reducing emergency response lead time and improving public safety outcomes during rapidly developing flood events.
4.6 Component 5 — Outfall Stabilization
The primary drainage outfall at the Ohio River backwater interface will be armored with engineered rip-rap to prevent erosion and scour during high-velocity return flow events following flood recession. Return-flow velocities at the outfall location present significant erosion risk that, if unaddressed, could progressively undermine berm integrity and drainage structure foundations over the project's operational life. Rip-rap sizing will be determined through hydraulic analysis per USACE EM 1110-2-1601 guidance, using the design peak discharge of approximately 142,000 cfs at the Ohio/Wabash confluence during the 100-year event.

8.0 Benefit-Cost Analysis Summary
8.1 BCA Methodology
The Benefit-Cost Analysis (BCA) for the Archimedes Line Phase I project has been conducted in accordance with FEMA BCA Toolkit guidance and OMB Circular A-94, using annualized expected damage avoidance as the primary benefit metric. Annualized expected losses are calculated by integrating flood damage estimates across the full probability distribution of flood events, weighted by annual exceedance probability. All monetary values are expressed in 2026 dollars. A discount rate of 7% per annum is applied in accordance with OMB Circular A-94 requirements for federal benefit-cost analyses. 8.3 Benefit-Cost Ratio Calculation
The BCR of 1.41 demonstrates that for every dollar of federal investment in the Archimedes Line Phase I project, $1.41 in quantified, avoided damages and economic losses are returned to the community, to property owners, to agricultural operators, and to public emergency management agencies. This ratio is considered conservative, as it does not fully monetize several additional categories of benefit, including: avoided loss of life and personal injury, reduced psychological trauma and displacement costs among flood-affected households, avoided contamination from flood-displaced agricultural chemicals, long-term ecosystem services provided by the engineered retention basin network, and the enhanced data value generated by the expanded PTDT v32 sensor network.
