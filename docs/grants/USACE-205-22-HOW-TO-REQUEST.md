# How Posey County Would Request USACE Section 205 / PAS Section 22 Studies

**What this is:** the actual request mechanics from the official USACE fact
sheets on file (`~/workspace/tsm-forms/grants/`, extracted 2026-10-06):
Section 205 Small Flood Risk Management Projects (2025) and Planning
Assistance to States / Section 22 (February 2025). Only what the documents
state — no inferred steps.

> USACE = U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. For Posey County, the relevant office
> is the **Louisville District**. Find districts at usace.army.mil/Locations.aspx.

## Section 205 — small flood damage reduction projects

**What it is:** the Corps partners with a local government "sponsor" to plan
and build small flood projects (levees, floodwalls, diversion channels, pumping
plants, bridge modifications — or non-structural: floodproofing, relocations,
flood warning systems). Only for projects **not** already authorized by Congress
and not part of a larger project. Authority: Section 205, Flood Control Act of
1948 (P.L. 80-858), as amended.

**How to request (from the fact sheet):**
1. A potential sponsor sends a **written Letter of Intent (LOI)** to the Corps.
   A template LOI is on the USACE Planning website.
2. When funding is available, the Corps does a **Federal Interest Determination
   at federal expense** — deciding whether the project meets program
   requirements and federal participation is justified.
3. If federal interest is verified, a **feasibility study** follows: alternatives
   evaluated, a recommended plan. If the study costs more than $100,000, both
   sides sign a Feasibility Cost Share Agreement + project management plan.
4. If the study finds the project technically feasible, environmentally
   acceptable, and cost-effective, both sides negotiate a **Project Partnership
   Agreement**, then design and construction begin.

**Who writes the letter:** the sponsor — for Posey County, that means the
**Board of Commissioners** (or a city/town council for a municipal project).
Individuals cannot sponsor.

**Cost shares (from the fact sheet):**
| Phase | Federal | Sponsor |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Federal Interest Determination | 100% | — |
| Feasibility study, first $100,000 | 100% | — |
| Feasibility study above $100,000 | 50% | 50% (cash, work-in-kind, or both) |
| Design & construction | 65% | 35% (can rise toward 50% with high land/easement costs) |
| **Maximum federal per project** | **$15,000,000** (study + design + construction) | |

Sponsor must: provide **all** lands, easements, rights-of-way, relocations, and
disposal areas (LERRDs); at least **5% of its share in cash**; and pay **all
operation and maintenance** after completion.

## Planning Assistance to States (Section 22) — studies and plans

**What it is:** Corps **planning-level assistance** — comprehensive water
resources plans or technical assistance (hydrologic, economic, environmental
data and analysis). Authorized by Section 22, WRDA 1974 (42 U.S.C. §1962d–16).

**Key limits (from the fact sheet):**
- **50% federal / 50% non-federal** cost share.
- **No detailed design for construction**, and the partner implements the plan —
  the Corps does not build it.
- Comprehensive plans: max **$5M federal per state/Tribe/territory per fiscal year**.
- Technical assistance cost share must be **cash** (in-kind not permitted for
  technical assistance; in-kind allowed for comprehensive plans).
- Eligible partners: states, groups of states, local governments, regional
  coalitions, Tribes, territories; nonprofits working **with** a state.

**How to request:** contact the local District's **Planning Department**
(Louisville District for Posey County). Template PAS agreements are on the
Corps HQ website (Project Partnership Agreements / templates_pas/).

**Related — Floodplain Management Services (FPMS):** the Corps provides flood
technical services and planning guidance to state, regional, and local
governments **without charge**, within program funding limits. (From the same
fact sheet — the cheapest first step before any study.)

## What TSM provides for either request

- The evidence base a sponsor needs: flood maps, structure inventories,
  elevations with provenance, and the ERDC confluence model reference.
- TSM does not write the Letter of Intent and does not act as sponsor.
  The county (or city/town) decides, signs, and sponsors.
