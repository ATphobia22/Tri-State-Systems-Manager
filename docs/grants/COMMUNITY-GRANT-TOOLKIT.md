# Community Grant Toolkit — Posey County & Tri-State Families

**Plain-language guide.** Which grant fits which need, who can apply, and what it
takes. Written for families and communities, not engineers.

> Source: official program documents on file in `~/workspace/tsm-forms/grants/`
> (extracted 2026-10-06). Only requirements actually stated in those documents
> are listed below. Always confirm current details with the agency before acting.

## Quick match: "I need…"

| Need | Program | Who applies | Federal share |
|------|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Fix sewers, drinking water, storm drains in a small town | **USDA Water & Waste Disposal** | Town/county government, nonprofit, Tribe | Low-interest loan (up to 40 yrs); grant possible with loan |
| Stormwater/ditch upgrades in a rural town | **OCRA CDBG Stormwater (SIP)** | County, city, or incorporated town (townships go through the county) | Up to $750,000 grant, 10% local match |
| Sewer or water plant project | **OCRA CDBG Wastewater/Drinking Water (WDW)** | County, city, or incorporated town | Up to $750,000 grant, 20% local match |
| Clean up runoff, streambanks, wetlands | **IDEM Section 319** | Municipality, county, nonprofit 501(c)(3), university, state/federal agency | Grant; 40% local match (20% for planning) |
| A levee, floodwall, or flood study for our area | **USACE Section 205** | A government "sponsor" (county/city) writes a Letter of Intent | Study: first $100k federal; construction 65% federal / 35% local |
| A water-resources plan or technical study | **USACE Planning Assistance to States** | State, county/city, Tribe | 50% federal / 50% local |

## Who can apply — the important rule

- **OCRA CDBG:** only counties, cities, and incorporated towns. **Townships
  cannot apply directly** — an unincorporated area like Point Township must apply
  **through Posey County**. One application per round per program.
- **USDA Water & Waste:** towns of **10,000 or fewer people**; towns, counties,
  nonprofits, and Tribes may apply.
- **IDEM 319:** only the organization types on the form (municipality, county,
  nonprofit, university, state/federal government). If your organization type is
  not listed, you are not eligible.
- **USACE 205/22:** a government sponsor is required. Individuals cannot apply.
- **Federal grants generally:** the applicant needs a Unique Entity Identifier
  (UEI) and an **active SAM.gov registration** before applying.

## What each program requires (from the official documents)

**OCRA CDBG (2026 Round 2):**
- An **OCRA-certified Grant Administrator** must prepare and submit the application.
- Project must benefit **low- and moderate-income (LMI) residents** (stormwater,
  water/sewer, facilities) or eliminate **slum/blight** (blight clearance, main
  street). LMI is shown with Census data or an approved income survey.
- Local match: 10% of total project cost (blight, facilities, stormwater) or
  20% (main street, water/sewer). Match is a share of **total project cost**.
- Project must be completable within **18 months** of award.
- Cost per beneficiary capped at **$5,000** in CDBG funds.
- Two stages: a **proposal** (95% complete) first, then OCRA invites (or does not
  invite) a full application after a site visit.

**USDA Water & Waste Disposal:**
- Applications accepted **year-round** through RD Apply; start by contacting the
  local USDA Rural Development state office.
- Funds drinking water, sewer, solid waste, and **storm water drainage** systems.
- Loans are fixed-rate up to 40 years; interest rate depends on local median
  household income and project need.

**IDEM Section 319 (nonpoint source):**
- A **Notice of Intent (NOI)** must be approved first — no NOI, no application.
- Only the official **State Form 49367** may be used; altered forms are rejected.
- 40% of total project cost must come from the applicant (20% for developing a
  watershed management plan). Project term: max **48 months**.
- Only 4 attachments allowed. Quarterly progress reports required; 10–15% of
  funds held until the final report.
- American-made products required for construction (Build America, Buy America).

**USACE Section 205 (small flood projects):**
- The county or city sends a **written Letter of Intent** to the Corps (a
  template is on the USACE Planning website).
- The Corps first does a **Federal Interest Determination at federal expense**.
- If justified: feasibility study (first $100,000 federal; costs above that
  split 50/50), then design/construction at 65% federal / 35% sponsor.
- The sponsor provides all land/easements/rights-of-way and pays **all**
  operation and maintenance forever after.

**USACE Planning Assistance to States (Section 22):**
- 50/50 cost share for **planning and studies only** — no construction design,
  no construction. The community implements the plan itself.
- Separately, the Corps' **Floodplain Management Services** program gives free
  flood technical help to state and local governments (within funding limits).

## What TSM does and does not do

TSM **does not** submit grant applications — only governments and eligible
organizations apply. What TSM provides: the maps, elevations, flood data, and
project evidence a community needs to write a strong application.
