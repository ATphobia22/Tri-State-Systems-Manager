# ERDC/CHL TR-22-4: Wabash–Ohio Confluence Evidence Note

**Source:** Bell, Gary; David Abraham; Nate Clifton; and Kenneth H. Lamkin. *Wabash and Ohio River Confluence Hydraulic and Sediment Transport Model Investigation*. U.S. Army Engineer Research and Development Center, Coastal and Hydraulics Laboratory, ERDC/CHL TR-22-4, February 2022. Public release.

**Official report library:** https://erdclibrary.on.worldcat.org/discovery  
**Report identifier:** ERDC/CHL TR-22-4  
**Source status in TSM:** Research reference only. This note does not ingest the original model, bathymetry, or measured observations and does not constitute a local calibration.

## Findings reported by ERDC

- Wabash River channel avulsions in 2008 and 2010 transported sediment downstream and contributed to shoaling at and downstream of the Wabash–Ohio confluence, increasing navigation-channel dredging needs.
- ERDC used the two-dimensional Adaptive Hydraulics (AdH) shallow-water model linked to SEDLIB sediment-transport calculations.
- Phase 2 extended the Ohio River model from J. T. Myers Locks and Dam (river mile 846.2) downstream to Smithland Locks and Dam (river mile 918.5), approximately 72 river miles.
- The study used field observations including multibeam bathymetry, bed sediment samples, suspended sediment samples, and ADCP discharge/velocity measurements. The November 2018 observations were collected during rapidly changing flows, limiting direct time-matched dynamic validation.
- ERDC reported that modeled flow-split percentages were similar to measured percentages. Suspended-sediment comparisons varied by location; the Range 5 average model/measurement difference was approximately 51%, discussed in the report alongside substantial sampling variability. Bed gradations were reported to compare favorably at the three examined Ohio River locations.
- The report compared a two-year hydrograph (26 September 2015–26 September 2017) across the base condition and alternatives. Its preferred alternative, It2_PC330, used seven level-crested dikes: four on the Illinois bank with crest elevation 312 ft and three on the Wabash Island side with crest elevation 330 ft, in the report's Ohio River Datum. It was selected because modeled navigable-water results were nearly identical to an eight-structure alternative while requiring seven structures.
- ERDC's stated objective was navigation-channel and sediment-management improvement at this specific confluence—not a general regional flood-warning model.

## Engineering interpretation for Tri-State Systems Manager

This report is a useful precedent for evidence-based river hydraulics and sediment modeling, but its results must not be transferred directly to other reaches, parcels, flood events, or design decisions. The report's bathymetry, mesh, boundary conditions, calibration observations, roughness assignments, vertical datum, and structure geometry are site- and period-specific.

TSM may cite this report as **published engineering context** for:
1. documenting why bathymetry, sediment gradation, discharge, and velocity observations matter;
2. motivating explicit calibration and validation records for any future hydraulic model;
3. recording the importance of vertical-datum metadata and boundary-condition provenance; and
4. distinguishing navigation/sediment objectives from flood-inundation objectives.

The report alone does **not** authorize or validate a TSM flood simulation, establish current channel conditions, provide a current flood forecast, or support construction recommendations elsewhere in the tri-state region.

## Minimum evidence gates before using this study as model input

- Obtain and license/authorize the actual source datasets and model artifacts; a published report is not a substitute for them.
- Record dataset source, acquisition date, spatial coverage, coordinate reference system, vertical datum, units, and transformation provenance.
- Verify current terrain and bathymetry against authoritative, time-appropriate observations.
- Establish reach-specific boundary conditions and calibration/validation observations.
- Report quantitative fit metrics, uncertainty, sensitivity, and limitations; do not treat qualitative visual agreement as sufficient.
- Keep any unverified area of interest and model scenario explicitly **UNAPPROVED** until its boundary and input evidence are reviewed by a qualified authority.
