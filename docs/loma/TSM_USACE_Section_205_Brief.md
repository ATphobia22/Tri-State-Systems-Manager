# USACE Section 205 — Small Flood Risk Management (Brief)

**Authority:** Section 205, Flood Control Act of 1948, as amended (Continuing Authorities Program)

## What it is

A path for **small** flood risk management projects **without** a project-specific Act of Congress. Can include structural measures (e.g. small levees, channels) or nonstructural measures (e.g. elevation, floodproofing, acquisition), if justified.

## Who can start it

A **non-federal sponsor** empowered under state law (typically a city, county, or similar) — **not** an individual homeowner alone. Individual technical packages can support a sponsor’s request; they do not replace the sponsor.

## Cost share (typical published structure; confirm with Louisville District)

| Phase | Cost share |
|-------|------------|
| Feasibility — first ~$100,000 | 100% federal |
| Feasibility — above that | Often 50% federal / 50% sponsor |
| Design & construction | Often 65% federal / 35% sponsor (LERRDs + minimum cash rules apply) |
| Operation & maintenance after construction | Sponsor 100% |

Federal per-project caps are limited (district fact sheets cite on the order of **$10M–$15M** federal — **verify current ceiling** with Louisville District). All work is subject to **appropriations**.

## Decision tests USACE will apply

1. Federal interest  
2. Engineering feasibility  
3. Economic justification (benefits > costs)  
4. Environmental acceptability (NEPA)  
5. Completeness of project  

## Realistic timeline

Feasibility alone is often cited around **24–30 months** when funded — not days or weeks.

## Relation to TSM / restricted site

Section 205 is a **community-scale** path if Posey County (or another eligible sponsor) submits a formal request and can cost-share. TSM can supply documented flood history and elevation context. TSM **cannot** self-sponsor a Section 205 project as a private individual.

## Practical next step

County (or multi-county) letter of interest to **USACE Louisville District** CAP / Section 205 POC, attaching a short problem statement and willingness to discuss cost share — after local elected/floodplain coordination.
